# Newsletters and news briefings

Two different jobs share this channel. Don't apply one's length rule to the other.

| Job | What the reader needs | Length |
|-----|----------------------|--------|
| One offer (promo, nurture, a single ask) | One action | Short. The remix table in [persuasion-frameworks.md](persuasion-frameworks.md) is this job. |
| News briefing | What changed, and the decision it creates | As long as the new facts. Cut anything the reader already has. |

Subject-line length is not one number. Cold outbound and a briefing follow different evidence. The split, and the studies, live in [headlines.md](headlines.md). Front-load the number and the name so they survive the 30-50 characters a phone shows.

## The three lines that must match

The subject, the headline, and the preview text (the first 40-90 characters beside the subject) are three surfaces. Each one has to name the same fact.

- A subject about a filing deadline and a headline about a different order is a bait-and-pivot. Readers who open on one and land on the other stop trusting the next issue.
- The preview is a sentence. If it doesn't parse, it fails even when the body is clean.
- A clever headline that the reader has to translate fails the clarity test in headlines.md. The subject can carry the plain version. The headline still has to say the thing.

## First sentences

Axios's newsroom rule, from eye-tracking and read-time tests: the reader decides in well under a second whether this is for them, and spends about 26 seconds if they stay. Say what's new in one sentence. The next sentence says why it matters to this reader. Cut what is familiar.

A later section earns its place only by adding a fact the opening didn't already state. A section that restates the lead in a new label is the inline-header failure (rule #17), even when the label is "Bottom line" or "Why it matters."

## One clock, one instrument

A briefing that staples two dated authorities into one noun sends the reader to the wrong document. Name each instrument in its own clause: who issued it, the date, and what it actually requires. The editorial pass for this is pattern 12 in [editorial-edit-patterns.md](editorial-edit-patterns.md).

## Series

If yesterday's issue already carried a number, don't present it again as the news. A daily reader can see the template. Repeat a figure only when today's fact changes it.
